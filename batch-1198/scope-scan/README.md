The Linux timing job used one complete physical checkout. Each group had one warm-up and five scored rounds in fresh Python processes. Both planners produced the same complete plan with 199 roots and 656 original members. Applying that plan on all three tools recovered every source. The two PR revisions generated byte-identical output for all 199 roots in every trial. The job verified all 59,909 physical Git source blobs before and after the trials. The full planner held the scope stacks until it finished. Its median peak memory grew by 12,476,416 bytes and elapsed time increased in all five pairs. The revised saved apply took less elapsed and CPU time in every pair. MB is decimal. A separate Apple silicon comparison measured every one of the 11,551 edited clumps with its real member bodies. The sum of median render CPU times was 2.655 seconds on community main, 3.981 seconds on the preceding PR revision and 1.681 seconds on the final revision. Including member construction, these CPU times were 6.262, 7.787 and 5.477 seconds. Final construction alone took 3.790 seconds, compared with 3.599 on community main.
